# B2B Archetype Boundaries

Use this only when business model changes the recommendation. It is a diagnostic lens, not a label the user must adopt.

## Classification

Choose the closest primary archetype and report confidence. If the business spans models, name the secondary archetype and the boundary between them.

| Archetype | Commercial shape | Do not generalize from |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription software | Recurring access, adoption and retention compound, marginal delivery cost is usually low | Consumer apps, services utilization, or hardware channel economics |
| Industrial / physical | Physical production, inventory, distribution, installation, or procurement materially shapes demand | Pure-software funnel benchmarks or instant self-serve conversion assumptions |
| Expert services | Trust, expertise, capacity, scope, and utilization constrain growth | Product-led onboarding or unlimited scale assumptions |
| Platform / ecosystem | Multiple participant groups create value for one another; liquidity and governance matter | Single-buyer funnels that ignore supply-side health |
| Regulated financial | Trust, risk controls, eligibility, compliance, and unit economics constrain messaging and distribution | Unregulated growth tactics or unsupported outcome claims |
| Other / ambiguous | The model does not fit cleanly or evidence is insufficient | Any archetype-specific recommendation until assumptions are validated |

## Required output for strategic work

```text
Business model: [primary, optional secondary]
Confidence: [high | medium | low]
Evidence: [facts from product, customer, pricing, or distribution context]
Assumptions: [what remains inferred]
Boundary: [where advice from another archetype would break]
```

## Rules

- Classify from how value is created, delivered, paid for, and retained—not the company’s preferred category label.
- Do not infer economics from the archetype. Request or mark unknown the actual price, margin, sales cycle, retention, and channel data.
- Use archetypes to remove irrelevant tactics, not to manufacture certainty.
- Reclassify when evidence changes. A services-led company becoming software-led may require two concurrent models during transition.
