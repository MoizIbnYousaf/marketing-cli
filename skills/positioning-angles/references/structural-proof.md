# Structural Proof and Perception Gaps

A memorable angle is not automatically a defensible position. For each candidate, identify why the company can keep the promise:

- product architecture or workflow;
- proprietary or hard-won data;
- distribution access or ecosystem position;
- operating process, expertise, or service model;
- customer proof that is current, attributable, and representative.

If no structural support exists, label the angle a messaging hypothesis. Do not convert a slogan into a competitive claim.

Compare three views of the company:

1. **Internal view** — what the team believes is differentiated.
2. **Customer view** — what buyers actually notice, value, fear, or misunderstand.
3. **Operational view** — what the product and delivery system can consistently prove.

The strongest position sits where customer value and operational proof overlap. A gap between views is evidence to investigate, not copy to smooth over.

For B2B offers, test both sides:

- **Economic case:** what cost, revenue, time, risk, or capacity mechanism changes?
- **Buyer-risk case:** what makes choosing this credible and professionally safe for the decision-maker?
